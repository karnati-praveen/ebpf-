### Verified publisher-wide rules

- **Highlights, if the journal requests them:** exact official sentences: “Include 3 to 5 highlights.” and “Each individual Highlight should be a maximum of 85 characters long, including spaces.” A separate editable source file is the default; check the individual guide for exceptions. [Elsevier Support, specifications](https://www.elsevier.support/publishing/answer/how-do-i-include-highlights-with-my-manuscript).
- **Graphical abstract, if submitted:** official size excerpt: “please provide an image with a minimum of 1328 x 531 pixels (w x h)”. The current general page specifies 300 dpi minimum and TIFF/EPS/PDF/MS Office preferred formats; it does not establish whether any particular journal requires one. [Elsevier graphical abstract, Specifications](https://www.elsevier.com/researcher/author/tools-and-resources/graphical-abstract).
- **Research data:** exact official sentence: “Elsevier journals have different guidelines when it comes to research data, either encouraging or requiring you to take certain actions.” Options A/B encourage deposit and citation, with B additionally encouraging linking or an explanation; C requires deposit/citation/linking or an explanation; D requires deposit/citation/linking; E additionally requests data review. Which option applies to each requested journal is UNVERIFIED. Prepare a truthful statement and permanent artifact link, but do not describe universal mandatory open data. [Elsevier Research data guidelines, journal options](https://www.elsevier.com/researcher/author/tools-and-resources/research-data/data-guidelines).
- **AI:** exact official sentence: “Authors should disclose the use of AI tools for manuscript preparation in a separate AI declaration statement included in their manuscript upon submission.” The current policy explicitly includes agents and literature-synthesis assistance, requires human verification, and places the declaration immediately before references. It should name the tool, purpose, oversight, and human responsibility. Basic grammar/spelling/punctuation checks are exempt; substantive restructuring is not. AI used in the research method belongs in Methods. Do not list an AI as an author. This research-assistance session therefore needs disclosure if used in the manuscript. This is broader than older readability-only descriptions; use the current policy. [Elsevier Generative AI policies, Disclosure / where to disclose](https://www.elsevier.com/en-gb/about/policies-and-standards/generative-ai-policies-for-journals).

## Implications for Shardwise and venue choice

**Provisional target: Journal of Systems Architecture, after stronger hardware and baseline evaluation.** The publisher explicitly covers system software and parallel/distributed architectures, and recently accepted EdgeATP and PRAS closely match runtime scheduling at the edge. The scope also cautions: “Case studies are welcome but must contribute more than just a design for a particular piece of software.” This requires a reusable systems insight, not merely introducing a runtime. [JSA publisher scope, Description](https://shop.elsevier.com/journals/journal-of-systems-architecture/1383-7621).

FGCS is a defensible second choice for infrastructure/telemetry and adaptation: its scope includes distributed resource scheduling and infrastructure monitoring. [FGCS publisher scope](https://shop.elsevier.com/journals/future-generation-computer-systems/0167-739X). JPDC becomes stronger if the finite-work objective, predictor and placement limitations receive a substantial algorithmic/performance-analysis contribution. [JPDC publisher scope](https://shop.elsevier.com/journals/journal-of-parallel-and-distributed-computing/0743-7315). Internet of Things needs a concrete IoT/embedded use case or physical-device contribution; a generic VM chat service is weaker evidence for that positioning. [IoT publisher scope](https://shop.elsevier.com/journals/internet-of-things/2542-6605).

The sample provides **no universal minimum device count or repeat count**. One physical device can support a hardware/compiler paper, while simulated eight/36-device studies provide no physical cluster validation. SpectraBench's explicit single-run limitation prevents treating significance testing as universally required by acceptance. Nevertheless, matched baselines, workload coverage, physical hardware appropriate to the claim, and transparent experimental boundaries recur across the closest accepted examples. This is an inference from this sample, not a journal rule.

For Shardwise, 65 completed runs and 939 requests on two similar Azure VMs support a bounded empirical study, but cannot establish consumer-device behavior or 2–N scalability. Before a consumer-cluster submission, add a heterogeneous physical LAN deployment with at least a three-stage layout to exercise multi-worker topology, a larger model that makes capacity aggregation useful, and an optimized single-device baseline plus a feasible reproduced distributed baseline under identical precision/length/load. Measure actual node-loss recovery, interrupted/completed requests, TTFT/inter-token tails, startup and full transition disruption, and replay cost across context lengths. Assess the payback forecast on held-out matched-start finite cohorts; report run-level uncertainty and count failures. Choose repeats from pilot variance and an effect threshold, not from a fabricated journal requirement. Keep negative gate/adaptation outcomes. Public raw run logs/configurations/artifact revisions and a truthful data/AI statement improve inspectability without implying that a journal universally mandates their exact form.
