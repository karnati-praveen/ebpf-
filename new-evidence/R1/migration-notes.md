# Migration, recovery and claim audit

Checked 6 October 2026. This is the migration/recovery part of the bounded research search, not an exhaustive systematic review. The CSV distinguishes a measured mechanism from metadata-only discovery. `UNVERIFIED` means the specified detail was not established by the inspected source; it does not mean that the paper lacks the feature. Direct quotations occur only in the CSV to avoid repeating quotations across artifacts. Other facts are located paraphrases.

## Claims that prior art already takes

**Migration payback gate: taken at the generic level, including a remaining-generation horizon.** [DiSCo, Findings ACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.734/) §4.3, proceedings p14264/PDF p5, equation (4), requires predicted per-token cost savings times remaining output length to exceed migration overhead. That is substantially the same amortization principle claimed by a remaining-work migration gate. Its monetary/energy endpoint choice differs from Shardwise's layer-placement latency surrogate; that difference supports a scoped implementation comparison, not firstness of payback gating. [STAR, HPDC 2026, formerly ARES](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13668) §5.2/Algorithm 1 p9 excludes near-complete requests if their predicted remaining generation is insufficient to amortize migration time. Root researcher verified this independently. ATSInfer's minimum interval/deviation threshold and DynoPipe's 15–20% hysteresis also take generic suppression of gratuitous movement; those alone are weaker evidence than DiSCo/STAR for explicit payback.

**Replay-based KV reconstruction instead of transfer: taken.** [ServerlessLLM, OSDI 2024](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi24/presentation/fu) §5.2–5.3, proceedings p141, uses input and generated tokens to reconstruct KV at the destination rather than sending large KV tensors. It additionally implements multi-round live migration to limit interruption. Shardwise's full-chain replay after a contiguous-layer layout generation changes is a different implementation scope; replay itself is not a novel migration protocol. [ctHO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28018) jointly optimizes local token prefill and residual KV transfer; [Pallas](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16477) performs prefix reconstruction and suffix transfer before handover. [EdgeFlow](https://doi.org/10.1109/2575-8411.2026.00033) integrates direct KV transmission and activation-based recomputation, but detailed fulltext implementation is UNVERIFIED here. [DynoPipe](https://doi.org/10.1109/ISCA66397.2026.00077) §4.3 has an activation-recomputation fallback when bandwidth drops below 1 Gbps. These mechanisms must not be collapsed into the same replay algorithm: token replay, activation recomputation, and partial KV transfer have different costs.

**Measured recovery on real consumer devices: broad claim already published; Shardwise's own evidence still open.** [ShadowLLM hot standby, ICDCS 2026](https://doi.org/10.1109/2575-8411.2026.00051) explicitly evaluates Jetson AGX Orin, Jetson AGX Xavier and smartphones, and reports 6–14× recovery-latency reduction over reactive baselines. Publisher abstract and complete metadata are verified; exact device counts, models, failure protocol, baseline names and absolute values await fulltext. Do not substitute the unrelated EMNLP 2024 ShadowLLM sparsity paper. DéjàVu and SpotServe already measure distributed fault recovery on real cloud GPUs. Pallas measures real workstation-GPU handover recovery, which is different from unannounced device loss. The manuscript's two Azure VMs do not substantiate a consumer-device recovery claim regardless of prior art. A narrower new physical experiment could be a contribution, but cannot establish firstness of real-device recovery.

**Objective depends on concurrency: established principle, with very close 2026 evidence.** EdgeShard's sum versus bottleneck objectives already motivate the difference. [DynoPipe, ISCA 2026](https://yanyinglin.github.io/assets/pdf/DynoPipe-ISCA26.pdf) §4.2/PDF p8 and §5.5–5.6/PDF pp10–11 explicitly distinguish low load from near-capacity load, and show split-point changes with load and network. Its Llama2-7B matched split-point experiment at QPS5 reports 292 versus 478 ms average latency and 3.43 versus 2.09 requests/s for SP12 versus cloud-only; it explains the gain through reduced queueing despite added traversal overhead. §5.2 also reports worse TPOT but better service throughput. FlexPipe combines latency and throughput in a runtime stage-granularity score and adapts to workload variability. PipeLive v2 discusses workload-dependent PP placement while leaving the high-level target selection policy outside its mechanism. A Shardwise concurrency-one/two reversal can still be an artifact-backed observation in its CPU runtime, not a new concurrency principle.

## Claims that remain narrower empirical questions

**Telemetry contaminated by queueing: the broad observation is established; the exact instrumented residual mechanism remains a possible scoped diagnostic contribution.** Llumnix explicitly models queue pressure and shows how queueing dominates tail latency. ServerlessLLM separates loading queue time from transfer/loading time (§6.1, p142), uses per-server loading queues to reduce contention, and warns that contention complicates bandwidth estimation. DynoPipe §5.6/PDF p11 separates queueing, edge compute, network and cloud compute; at QPS5 its cloud-only queue is 295 ms/62% of end-to-end latency, versus 76 ms/26% for SP12. This does not itself prove a prior identical subtraction of `RPC elapsed − reported worker compute` into a supposed link estimate. Safe wording: Shardwise quantifies how that implementation's residual includes worker lock wait in controlled loopback windows. It must not claim to discover that application latency includes queueing, and its loopback experiment does not establish the same 99.2% fraction on Azure/consumer links.

**Outbound-only multi-device joining with no inbound joiner ports: precise firstness unproven.** This migration search did not verify a matching peer-reviewed application-layer protocol. Connex supports restricted-network proxy-mediated transport and dynamic endpoint joining, but inbound-port avoidance is UNVERIFIED. [LocalAI's official ds4 distributed documentation](https://localai.io/docs/features/distributed-mode/) explicitly makes workers dial a coordinator; however, control connection direction is insufficient evidence that every data channel is outbound-only. The upstream protocol advertises worker data ports and allows direct worker connections. Thus this is a threat to loose “worker-initiated joining” language, not a verified counterexample to the full no-inbound-port property. Existing relay/NAT traversal in volunteer inference and established reverse connections make architectural novelty doubtful. Shardwise must demonstrate the exact property with firewall/NAT experiments, 3+ joiners, disconnect/reconnect and failure recovery, and compare peer-to-peer versus hub traffic. The current manuscript's controller-issued RPC architecture alone does not demonstrate outbound-only operation.

## New closest runtime reconfiguration papers

- [PipeLive arXiv 2604.12171v2, 23 September 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12171): live in-place contiguous PP remapping, KV resizing and incremental patching; separate A100/L40S nodes plus up to12 A100 GPUs. Latest body gives <1.5 ms service stop and 2.1–3.1 s total migration; the indexed v1 abstract's <10 ms and 54.7% improvement figures must not be mixed with v2.
- [ReMP arXiv 2606.18741v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18741): runtime TP/PP switching with reusable model state and two-dimensional KV transfer, on one8-H100 server. Latest matrix gives 1.0–7.4 s switching and approximately 2.5–25.8× restart speedups, not a blanket hundreds-fold result. Single multiGPU node is not a consumer network.
- [FlexPipe, EuroSys 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3767295.3769316): inflight pipeline refinement/consolidation on42 servers/82 GPUs; workload/CV-driven stage granularity and preserved KV transitions. General runtime repartitioning is taken.
- [OrionInfer, KDD 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3770855.3817626): metadata verified but fulltext blocked. Do not invent hardware, migration cost, failure handling or headline performance from its title.
- [Connex, SIGCOMM 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3789240.3829200): dynamic communication endpoint membership/handover, not itself a layer partition optimizer.5 servers/20A40 GPUs; communication continuity is established but application recovery obligations remain.

## Evidence limits and recommended comparison framing

All numbers are source-reported in their own setup; no system was reproduced. Shardwise's Qwen0.6B CPU results must not be ranked numerically against 7–176B GPU systems. The verified table preserves whether evaluation is hardware, simulated, trace-replayed, or a mixture. In particular Splitwise's cluster-scale headline results are simulation backed by hardware profiling, ctHO is simulation, and ImpactHO combines RULER/real inference calibration with a simulated handover scheduler.

No paper in this search supports a blanket firstness claim for the conjunction of every Shardwise feature. Conversely, failure to find the conjunction is not evidence of novelty. A useful journal contribution would hold starting layout, arrivals and mechanism fixed; sweep remaining workload around measured break-even; compare ungated/hysteresis/DiSCo- or STAR-style gating; and evaluate3–5 physically heterogeneous consumer devices with recovery and outbound-only firewall evidence.
