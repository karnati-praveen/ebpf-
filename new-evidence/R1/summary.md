# Shardwise related-work evidence — R1

**Recommendation: target Journal of Systems Architecture (JSA), after strengthening the evaluation.** Its systems architecture scope and recent [EdgeATP article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sysarc.2026.103861) make it the clearest fit for a measured implementation of distributed Transformer inference. EdgeATP is also direct competition: the paper must explain the distinction between adaptive tensor allocation and Shardwise's contiguous-layer replay and recovery. This is a scope recommendation, not a claim that the current submission would be accepted.

The evidence package contains **58 work rows** covering peer-reviewed papers, credible preprints and separately labelled software, plus **87 BibTeX entries** covering those works, accepted-paper calibration and publisher policies. Three research agents worked in parallel. The requested source files were read first. Metadata was checked against publisher proceedings, Crossref and arXiv; downloaded PDFs and metadata are retained under `sources/`. The sweep includes newer PipeLive, ReMP, DynoPipe, STAR, ShadowLLM, E2LLM, Pallas, LOIP and AI PC Fleets. Publication versions matter: EdgeShard and TPI-LLM are 2025 journal articles; prima.cpp is ICLR2026 with an expanded author list; STAR replaces the earlier ARES title.

**Most broad novelty claims are taken.** EdgeShard and other pipeline systems distinguish latency and throughput objectives. DiSCo gates migration on remaining decode savings exceeding overhead; STAR filters requests unable to amortize migration cost. ServerlessLLM reconstructs KV by replaying tokens, and Petals reconstructs lost stage caches by replaying activations. ShadowLLM reports recovery on Jetsons and smartphones; EASTER disables boards in an eight-Jetson cluster, although its continued inference is approximate. Petals supports firewalled participants through relays. Queueing and remote compute contaminating apparent network latency are established concerns, including an explicit instrumentation correction in AI PC Fleets. These papers differ in mechanisms and hardware; the distinctions do not justify blanket priority claims.

Shardwise's defensible position is a reproducible small-model CPU system combining adaptive contiguous partitioning, explicit replay accounting and careful telemetry analysis. **The existing evidence is two similar Azure VMs**, 65 completed runs and 939 requests, rather than a heterogeneous consumer-cluster recovery evaluation. The optional remaining-work gate has no established performance gain. Current workers listen for inbound inference connections, so outbound-only joining cannot be claimed from the implementation.

Before submission, add heterogeneous physical consumer machines and a configuration larger than two nodes; matched optimized-static and adaptive comparisons; profitable and unprofitable migration cases; abrupt machine loss with token-history preservation; long-history replay costs; and repeated measurements with uncertainty. Journal samples do not establish mandatory device counts or repeat counts.
